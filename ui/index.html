The political landscape, as the votes describe it

Every point is one Member of the European Parliament, placed by how they voted on every roll-call vote of the term. Nobody assigned these positions — they fall out of the voting record itself.

What separates the axes

The votes whose outcome most distinguishes members along each axis. An axis has no inherent direction: only distances and groupings mean anything, never "left" or "right" of zero.

Members

Search by name, country or political group. Figures are roll-call participation: Parliament does not publish a record of who was absent.

What the axes are made of

The political space is not drawn from any theory: the axes are simply the patterns that best separate how members voted. Each has no inherent direction — what gives an end its meaning is which votes a member is voting for when they sit there.

Topics

How often each political group's majority voted in favour, by subject area. Subjects are Parliament's own classification, not ours, and cover substantive votes from 2019 onwards.

Stories

Written by people, about what the votes show. Every number in them is computed from the verified record at publish time — but the readings between the numbers are arguments, and they are marked as such.
