Across the 2019-2024 Parliament there were {{votes}} substantive roll-call votes filed
under asylum and refugees. On those votes the governing centre held together about as
firmly as you would expect: the Socialists backed them {{sd}} of the time, Renew
{{renew}}, and the European People's Party {{epp}}.

## The interesting part is the other end

A one-dimensional reading of Parliament would predict that opposition to asylum
legislation comes from the right, and some of it plainly does: the European
Conservatives and Reformists voted in favour only {{ecr}} of the time, and Identity and
Democracy {{id}}.

But the Greens supported those same votes just {{greens}} of the time, and the Left
{{left}} — both well below the centre, and the Left below the ECR.

**That pattern is in the data. What it means is not.** A vote against a text can be a
vote against its existence or a vote against its inadequacy, and a roll-call record
cannot tell the two apart. Reading the Left's {{left}} and the ECR's {{ecr}} as the same
position would be a mistake, and it is exactly the mistake a single left-right axis
invites.

## Cohesion is not agreement

On these votes the EPP voted with its own majority {{epp_cohesion}} of the time — high,
but that is a statement about discipline, not about the merits. The non-attached
members, who are not a political group at all but a residual category, sit at
{{ni_cohesion}}, which is roughly what you would expect from a label that describes an
absence of affiliation rather than a shared position.

## What to check

Every figure above is computed from votes verified against Parliament's own record; none
of them were typed into this text. The readings between them — that opposition came from
two directions, that cohesion is not agreement — are ours, and you are entitled to
disagree with them while accepting the numbers.
